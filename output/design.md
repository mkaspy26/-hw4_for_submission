# Campus Customs: Storefront Design (Problem 10)

## The idea: "The Ancient Secrets of Yale"

Everyone who imagines Yale imagines the same thing: Gothic towers rising out of the mist, fog over old stones, lit windows in a library that looks like a cathedral, and whispers of secret societies. It's the feeling of being inside a Harry Potter film, and it's what people fantasize about when they picture life at Yale. The redesign is built so that shoppers feel they're **experiencing the ancient secrets of Yale**, as if they had walked onto that film set on a misty morning.

That feeling is the business case. A plain merch grid gives people a reason to buy and leave. A storefront that feels like a hidden, slightly spooky corner of Yale gives them a reason to **explore**: to hunt for the next hidden key, unlock the next secret in the Grimoire, read the next note from the Head's Study, flip the next college crest, and summon the guardian spirit in the corner. Every one of those moments keeps a shopper on the site longer. The longer they stay among the products, and the more **WOW'd** they are by the experience, the more likely they are to buy a piece of it to take home.

---

## 1. Colour: Yale navy on white and pale silver mist

- The site is **white**, washed with pale silver-blue mist (`#ffffff` → `#eef2f8` → `#dde5f0`), with **Yale navy (`#00356b`)** for type, silhouettes, buttons, and the nav and footer bands. There's no glow and no other colours, apart from soft warnings on stock badges.
- **Why:** the colours reflect the school's values. Yale navy is depth, steadiness, and tradition, the colour of *Lux et Veritas*, of the stands at The Game, and of a lifetime of belonging. White is light and truth, the clarity the motto promises. Keeping strictly to the school's own colours makes the shop feel official and trustworthy, and the mist keeps it mysterious without making it dark or hard to shop.
- *(Revision history: a fully dark midnight theme was tried and replaced. It was too heavy for browsing products, so the mystery now comes from fog and silhouettes on white.)*

## 2. Typography: legible, but ancient

| Role | Font | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Brand, headings, product names, buttons, nav | **IM Fell English SC** | Small capitals printed on an old letterpress, like the title page of a centuries-old book from Sterling's stacks |
| Subtitles, whispers, teasers | **IM Fell English** *(italic)* | A handwritten aside in the margin of an ancient tome |
| Letters, Head of College notes, secrets | **Cormorant Garamond** | An elegant letter read by candlelight |
| Prices, sizes, forms, chat text | **Inter** | Clean and modern, so anything a shopper must act on is instantly readable |

- **Why:** the IM Fell fonts are reproductions of 17th-century English type. They have the ink-bleed and irregularity of old printing (the "ancient, Harry Potter" feel), but they're true book faces, so they stay **easy to read** even in a product name or a nav link. Prices and forms stay in Inter, so the magic never gets in the way of buying.
- *(Revision history: Cinzel (too stiff, "movie poster") → Playfair Display (too clean and modern for the mystical direction) → IM Fell English.)*

## 3. The homepage: the old campus in the mist

- A **misty Gothic campus** fills the screen in layered silhouettes that grow from pale silver in the distance to deep navy in front:
  - a ridge of far-off spires;
  - college halls with rows of pointed lancet windows;
  - a library nave with a **rose window**;
  - a great **Harkness-like tower** over an arched gate;
  - a **wrought-iron fence** with spear-tipped railings in the foreground.
- **It's alive, and a little eerie:**
  - windows **flicker** as if candles burn behind the glass, and the rose window pulses;
  - white **fog drifts** across in three layers at different speeds, swallowing the bottom of the buildings;
  - specks of dust drift up, **ravens** cross the sky, and a pale moon hangs over the towers.
- "Campus Customs" is set in old-book capitals over the scene: *"When the fog rolls in over the old stones and the tower windows flicker, the regalia of Yale awaits those who know where to look."* Two calls to action: **Enter the Collection** and **Chat with Sir Handsome Dan**.
- A navy ribbon slowly scrolls the names of all **fourteen residential colleges** along the foot of the scene, with **Sir Handsome Dan running back and forth along it**, pausing to look at you. **Hover over any college and the ribbon pauses** while a little card pops up with that college's crest and story (e.g. *"Jonathan Edwards College: Named for the theologian Jonathan Edwards, a Yale graduate."*).
- Nothing sits in the middle of the view, so the scene and the title are unobstructed.
- Below it:
  - **A Welcome, by Candlelight:** a framed, wax-sealed letter from the Head's Study.
  - **The Fourteen:** college crests that flip to reveal their stories.
  - **Relics of the Vault:** favourite pieces.
  - **Come Find Us:** 57 Broadway.
- **Why:** the first five seconds decide whether someone stays. A cinematic, fog-wrapped opening shot, like the moment the camera first finds the castle through the mist, makes the "WOW" immediate. *(Revision history: blue night sky + candles → paper-style engraving → dark midnight scene → this misty white version.)*

## 4. Writing in the voice of a Head of College

- **Every one of the 102 products** carries a note *"From the Head's Study"* on its product page. It's written like a warm, slightly grand Head of College addressing their students ("Pierson men and women…", "Branford friends…"), and each note references one of the fourteen colleges.
  - **Branford Quarter-Zip example:** *"…Carved from the Memorial Quadrangle, Branford has Harkness Tower rising from its courtyard; the crest seems quite at home among such venerable masonry."*
- The homepage letter and the About page ("A Letter from the Head's Study") are written in the same voice.
- **How it stays accurate:** the notes were written once by the AI from a short, checked fact sheet about each college, and an automatic filter rejected any note that mentioned price, size, or stock (0 of 102 were rejected). Real product facts still come straight from the database and are shown separately.
- **Why:** shoppers read a story instead of a spec sheet. A Davenporter sees their own college addressed, and alumni hear the voice of the place they remember.

## 5. Hidden secrets: keys and the Grimoire (now much clearer)

- **Sixteen skeleton keys** are hidden across the site:
  - in the homepage motto, the Head's letter, beside "The Fourteen", by the shop's address, and beside Dan's portrait;
  - inside the About letter and next to the Collection's title;
  - on every product page;
  - at the Log In and Create Account doors;
  - in the footer;
  - inside Handsome Dan's chat.
- **Each key is easy to spot.** It sits in a small navy-outlined badge with a **pulsing ring** that ripples outward, and on hover it reads *"A secret stirs here… click the key"*.
- Clicking a key **unlocks** a real piece of Yale lore: Handsome Dan I as America's first live college mascot (1889), *Lux et Veritas*, the Beinecke's glowing marble walls, the 54 bells of Harkness, the Whiffenpoofs, the Yale Bowl, the Frisbie pie-tin legend, Louis' Lunch, the "tombs" of the secret societies, and more. Unlocked keys dim, so you know which you've found.
- **The Grimoire:** the **"🗝 3/16 secrets"** counter in the nav (and a link in the footer) opens a book of all sixteen secrets.
  - Unlocked secrets show their full fact.
  - Sealed ones show a **hint about where the key is hidden** (*"Hint: At the door marked Log In."*).
  - A progress bar fills as you go, and completing it shows *"The Grimoire is complete."*
- **Why:** the hunt is now a clear, satisfying game rather than guesswork. Hints send people to pages they haven't visited (About, product pages, the sign-up page), which is more exploration, more products seen, and more time on site. *(Revision history: ✦ stars, then paw prints. Both were too subtle, so they became glowing keys plus the Grimoire.)*

## 6. Sir Handsome Dan, the shopping guide

- **His look is modelled on a classic bulldog mascot, aged into a grown-up, and made majestic.** He has the same white coat, tan ears and brow patches, and thick navy mascot outlines.
  - He sits **front-facing and symmetrical with his chin up**, which reads as dignified rather than goofy.
  - He has **calm, heavy-lidded eyes glancing to the side**, a noble brow ridge, wide heavy jowls, a broad nose, and a composed mouth with a small underbite.
  - He wears a **small crown set slightly askew** and a **navy cape trimmed in ermine** over a broad white chest, with a white **"Y" medallion** on his collar.
  - There's no glow; he's a clean, confident mascot on white.
  - *(Revision: a three-quarter, side-turned head looked lopsided at small sizes, so his body and head now face forward and only his eyes glance aside.)*
- **He runs along the college ribbon.** The scrolling ribbon of the fourteen colleges is his floor.
  - A side-view Sir Handsome Dan (stocky body, big flat-faced bulldog head, crown tipped back by the wind) **gallops in from the left**, legs pumping, ears flapping, and body bobbing.
  - He **stops to sit and look at you** ("Oh, hello! Click me to chat"), runs on, stops once more, then **runs off the edge of the screen**.
  - A few seconds later he **comes back from the other side**, running the other way, pauses to look at you again, and exits. Then the loop repeats.
  - He's clickable at every moment, and he sits still for shoppers whose system asks for reduced motion.
- **He's always clickable.** Every Sir Handsome Dan on the site opens the chat:
  - the one in the corner of every page;
  - the **Dan running along the college ribbon** ("Chat with me!");
  - the large Dan in "Come Find Us";
  - the Dan on the **"Chat with Sir Handsome Dan"** homepage button;
  - the Dan on **"Ask Sir Handsome Dan about this item"** on every product page.
  - Even his face in the chat header can be clicked: he licks the screen again.
- A speech bubble periodically pops out of the corner Dan: *"Click me to chat! I can find sizes, colours & stock"*, so nobody wonders what he's for.
- **Click him and he licks the screen:** he leans in, his crown tips, his tongue slurps out the side of his mouth twice, a soft lick-smear wipes across the glass, and little sparks drift up. *Then* the chat opens.
- Inside, the chat is his:
  - his face in the header ("Sir Handsome Dan · Your Campus Customs shopping guide");
  - a courtly greeting: *"Good day. I am Sir Handsome Dan, keeper of the Campus Customs collection… How may I be of service?"*;
  - paw prints while he works (*"🐾 Dan is sniffing the shelves…"*);
  - the ready-made question chips;
  - search results labelled **"👑 Chosen for you by Sir Handsome Dan"**.
- **His voice:** a wise, courtly old bulldog knight: gracious, composed, quietly witty, with a hint of mystery. He uses at most one courtly touch per message, the occasional "paws-itively", and drops the ceremony when a shopper is frustrated or the question is serious. He is **never called a prince** anywhere on the site. The prompt tells him to refer to himself only as Sir Handsome Dan (tested: *"A prince? No, thankfully. Just a dignified old bulldog in service to Yale gear."*).
- **Why:** a shop chatbot only helps if people use it. Making *every* Dan clickable, with clear labels ("Chat with Sir Handsome Dan", "Click me to chat!"), removes any doubt about what he does. A grown-up, slightly regal version of the familiar bulldog mascot feels like Yale's own Handsome Dan, which makes chatting with him fun rather than annoying.

## 7. The secret society: Log In and Create Account

- **Create Account** is **"An Invitation · Join the Society"**, under a pulsing wax seal with a key.
  - *"Every great society begins with a name written in a ledger. Inscribe yours, and the halls will know you. What follows, we may neither confirm nor deny."*
  - Then, in plain words, a small **"Why create an account?"** box: Sir Handsome Dan remembers your chats so you can pick up where you left off (your sizes, the items you liked), he greets you by name when you return, and it's free and takes 30 seconds.
  - A whisper beneath: *"…and some members, they say, are one day tapped."*
  - The button reads **Inscribe My Name**.
- **Log In** is **"Members Only · Return to the Halls"**.
  - *"The doors remember those who have passed through them. Speak your name and your word, and they will open once more."*
  - A whisper fades in and out beneath: *"It is whispered that on certain spring evenings, a knock comes at the doors of a chosen few…"*
  - The button reads **Enter**, and the sign-up link reads **Seek membership**.
- **Why:** signing up is usually the most boring step on any site. The plain-language box makes the real benefit obvious in five seconds. Framing it as being invited into a mysterious society, a nod to Yale's real (and very famous) senior societies, makes it intriguing. More accounts means more returning shoppers that Dan can remember and greet by name. The teasers stay playful ("they say", "neither confirm nor deny") and promise nothing real.

## 8. Motion and product presentation

- **Products are framed in Gothic arches** on white cards that lift when hovered. Images zoom gently, and a "View details →" bar slides up. Names and prices are set in IM Fell capitals.
- **The product page** sets the photo in a double-bordered arch, and the Head's note sits in a quiet inset beside the facts.
- **Motion is slow and atmospheric:** fog drifts, windows flicker, dust rises, ravens pass, Dan floats and blinks, keys ripple, whispers fade in and out, crests turn in 3D, and the Grimoire fades in. Nothing flashes or blocks the shopper.

---

## Why this sells more

1. **Time on site goes up.** Keys to find, a Grimoire to fill, notes to read, crests to flip, and a guardian to summon all give people reasons to keep exploring, and every extra minute is spent among the products.
2. **Emotion drives purchase.** The site sells the *feeling* of Yale, the misty, Harry-Potter-like fantasy, and a hoodie becomes a way to take that feeling home.
3. **The assistant gets used.** Sir Handsome Dan is everywhere, clearly clickable, and fun to talk to, so shoppers ask more, and every answered size or stock question removes a reason not to buy.
4. **More sign-ups.** "Join the Society" turns account creation into intrigue, which brings more returning, remembered customers.
5. **Brand trust.** Strict Yale navy and white, legible type for every price and size, and accurate facts make it feel like the real, official home of Yale regalia.

All of this is built to increase time on the website and the likelihood of buying merchandise, because shoppers are **WOW'd by the experience**.
